# The UI aims for calm clarity; Plane is only the structural base

ADR 0003 said the web app must look and behave like Plane. The reason Plane was chosen was practical: it is open source and it shows a working project-management layout, not that Plane's look is the goal. The product owner's intent is a tool that can do many things yet stays easy to use and uncluttered, in the spirit of Height (a product that has closed, so only its idea is a reference, not its screens). Following Plane's look literally produced a dense screen where every action is a visible button.

From now on Plane supplies the structure (app rail, contextual sidebar, top bar, URL shapes, the Layouts, the vocabulary of Views, Cycles, Modules, Intake and Wiki) and the rule in ADR 0003 still holds: no Plane source and no `@makeplane/propel`. The look follows the principles in `DESIGN.md` under "Direction": capability stays, clutter goes. A screen shows one primary action and keeps the rest one step away (a menu, or on hover and focus, always reachable by keyboard and by touch); borders and boxes separate only where they carry meaning; colour is reserved for the primary action, focus and status.

We rejected keeping the Plane look as the goal (nobody wanted to defend its density) and copying Linear's or Height's visuals (their brand, and Linear's hover-only actions fail on touch). Changing a surface to follow the principles is cheap to reverse per screen, but the direction is what a reader of ADR 0003 would otherwise trip over, so it is recorded here. This decision narrows ADR 0003; it does not remove its licensing rule.
